# H4 Verification — Screener B (independent second pass)

**Hypothesis H4:** "In some types of non-Alzheimer's, non-aged human brain cells, APOE4 adheres more tightly to some recycling receptors than APOE3, impeding recycling."

**Verification criterion applied:** the paper is an experimental study that directly tests APOE4 vs APOE3/APOE2 (or APOE4 manipulation) on APOE4 binding/adhesion to a recycling receptor (ApoER2/LRP family, sortilin, retromer cargo receptors, LRP1) impeding receptor recycling — i.e. an isoform-specific apoE–receptor binding-affinity measurement, an apoE4–receptor complex/co-trafficking experiment, or a receptor-recycling blockade that the abstract attributes to apoE4–receptor interaction/adhesion (including delayed dissociation/trapping). Applied strictly: a paper that measures recycling impairment by APOE4 *without* any binding/interaction attribution in the abstract does not confirm — it is disputed (held out). Verification was made from the abstracts in `work/searches2/H4.json` and `work/searches2/H4r2.json` only (deduped union, 366 records, re-derived independently and matching Screener A's pool).

**Starting-reference verdict (prominent):** Xian X et al., eLife 2018, PMID 30375977, DOI 10.7554/elife.40048, "Reversal of ApoE4-induced recycling block as a novel prevention approach for Alzheimer's disease" — **present in the pool: YES. Meets eligibility: YES — CONFIRMED, ranked #1.** It directly measures the ApoE4-induced recycling block of the ApoE receptor Apoer2/Lrp8 (and of the AMPA/NMDA receptors co-endocytosed in complex with Apoer2) and its complete reversal by NHE6 inhibition, against the stated ApoE4-vs-ApoE3 single-amino-acid contrast, attributing the trafficking defect to ApoE4 unfolding on endosomal acidification. One honest qualification: the abstract reports no binding-affinity assay of its own — the adhesion mechanism (unfolded ApoE4 failing to release its receptor) is articulated as delayed dissociation explicitly in Pohlkamp 2021 (confirmed #2 below), from the same programme. The paper nonetheless directly tests the receptor-recycling block that H4 describes, so it confirms.

## Confirmed

1. DOI: 10.7554/elife.40048 — 2018 — Xian X — "Reversal of ApoE4-induced recycling block as a novel prevention approach for Alzheimer's disease." (Starting reference. Direct measurement and pharmacological/genetic reversal of the ApoE4-induced recycling block of Apoer2/Lrp8; mechanism attributed to ApoE4 unfolding on endosomal acidification.)
2. DOI: 10.7554/elife.72034 — 2021 — Pohlkamp T — "NHE6 depletion corrects ApoE4-mediated synaptic impairments and reduces amyloid plaque load." (States the H4 adhesion mechanism verbatim: ApoE4's isoelectric point matches early-endosomal pH, "causing its delayed dissociation from ApoE receptors and hence impaired endolysosomal trafficking"; NHE6 depletion corrects the defect.)
3. DOI: 10.1016/j.cell.2024.10.027 — 2025 — Guo JL — "Decreased lipidated ApoE-receptor interactions confer protection against pathogenicity of ApoE and its lipid cargoes in lysosomes." (Only paper coupling both arms in one study: impaired LDLR binding of lipidated ApoE2 avoids the LDLR recycling defects seen with lipidated ApoE3/E4, and the Christchurch mutation likewise reduces LDLR binding. Note: the recycling defect groups ApoE3 with ApoE4 against ApoE2 — it establishes binding-strength → recycling-defect coupling for ApoE4 vs ApoE2, but does not separate ApoE4 from ApoE3 on binding.)
4. DOI: 10.1016/j.neuron.2017.09.003 — 2017 — Zhao N — "Apolipoprotein E4 Impairs Neuronal Insulin Signaling by Trapping Insulin Receptor in the Endosomes." (Abstract states apoE4 "interacts with insulin receptor and impairs its trafficking by trapping it in the endosomes" in primary neurons and apoE-targeted-replacement mice — explicit interaction attribution. Note: the insulin receptor is a recycling receptor but not a canonical apoE receptor of the ApoER2/LRP/sortilin list; confirmed on the strength of the explicit apoE4–receptor interaction → endosomal trapping mechanism.)
5. DOI: 10.1242/jcs.258894 — 2021 — Asaro A — "ApoE4 disrupts interaction of sortilin with fatty acid-binding protein 7 essential to promote lipid signaling." (Abstract states "apoE4 binding blocks sortilin-mediated sorting," vs the functional apoE3 pathway; sortilin is a named recycling/sorting receptor in the eligibility rule. Caveat: the measured readout is sortilin-mediated sorting of its cargo FABP7, not sortilin's own surface recycling — confirmed because the blockade is explicitly attributed to apoE4 binding, but it is the least direct of the five.)

## Disputed

1. **DOI: 10.1073/pnas.0914984107 — 2010 — Chen Y — "ApoE4 reduces glutamate receptor function and synaptic plasticity by selectively impairing ApoE receptor recycling."**
   Reason: genuinely arguable both ways. For inclusion: it directly measures isoform-specific impairment of Apoer2 recycling — reduced neuronal surface Apoer2 (and NMDA/AMPA receptors) by sequestration in intracellular compartments in human-APOE knock-in mice and cultured neurons; it is the foundational recycling paper for this hypothesis. Against, under the strict rule: the abstract attributes the block to no binding/interaction mechanism at all — no binding, dissociation, complex or adhesion language; sequestration phenomenology only. It is the paradigm case of "recycling impairment measured without binding attribution," so B holds it out as disputed rather than confirming on the title alone. A included it (ranked #3).

2. **DOI: 10.1073/pnas.1801612115 — 2018 — Prasad H — "Amyloid clearance defect in ApoE4 astrocytes is reversed by epigenetic correction of endosomal pH."**
   Reason: measures a real ApoE4-specific receptor-trapping phenotype — LRP1 trapped in intracellular compartments with loss of surface expression and Aβ clearance in ApoE4 vs ApoE3 astrocytes — but the abstract attributes the trap entirely to a non-binding mechanism: ε4-selective down-regulation of NHE6 (via HDAC4) causing aberrant endosomal acidification. No apoE4–LRP1 interaction, binding or dissociation is reported or invoked. Recycling/trafficking impairment without binding attribution, with an alternative mechanism stated in its place — disputed (held out). A included it (ranked #7) with the same caveat B relies on here.

3. **DOI: 10.1161/atvbaha.108.164863 — 2008 — Altenburg M — "Human LDL receptor enhances sequestration of ApoE4 and VLDL remnants on the surface of hepatocytes but not their internalization in mice."**
   Reason: the binding arm is fully met — a direct isoform comparison showing "the high affinity of apoE4 to the LDLR" (vs ApoE2) — but the recycling arm is not: the measured outcomes are sequestration of ApoE4/VLDL remnants on the hepatocyte surface and delayed *internalisation*; LDLR recycling itself is never measured, and the impeded step precedes internalisation rather than following it. System is peripheral (mouse hepatocytes expressing human LDLR), comparator is ApoE2 not ApoE3. Binding-without-recycling — disputed (held out). A included it (ranked #8).

## Borderline-excluded (A's exclusions independently re-read and upheld; no additions to the confirmed list)

1. DOI: 10.1523/jneurosci.1400-19.2019 — 2019 — Rawat V — "ApoE4 Alters ABCA1 Membrane Trafficking in Astrocytes." Closest exclusion in the pool: ApoE4 does trap a membrane protein (ABCA1) in late endosomes and impair its recycling, isoform-specifically. But ABCA1 is a lipid transporter, not a recycling receptor under the eligibility list, and the abstract attributes the trapping to ApoE4-driven ARF6 up-regulation and ApoE4 aggregation — no apoE–ABCA1 binding/interaction is shown. A's exclusion upheld (this is a core H1 paper, not H4).
2. DOI: 10.1126/scisignal.aaa6674 — 2015 — Lane-Donovan C — "Reelin protects against amyloid β toxicity in vivo." The ApoE4 impairment of Apoer2/Vldlr recycling appears only as cited prior work ("We have previously shown…"); this study itself tests Reelin knockout and Aβ toxicity and runs no recycling experiment. Exclusion upheld.
3. DOI: 10.1016/j.jbc.2021.100715 — 2021 — Cooper JM — "Regulation of tau internalization, degradation, and seeding by LRP1 reveals multiple pathways for tau catabolism." Does show apoE4 as the most potent apoE inhibitor of LRP1-mediated tau uptake, "likely because of its higher affinity for LRP1" — a genuine isoform binding-affinity signal — but the endpoint is tau internalisation/degradation/seeding; LRP1 recycling impairment is not measured or claimed. Binding without a recycling readout. Exclusion upheld.
4. DOI: 10.1523/jneurosci.3993-06.2007 — 2007 — He X — "Apolipoprotein receptor 2 and X11 alpha/beta mediate apolipoprotein E-induced endocytosis of amyloid-beta precursor protein and beta-secretase, leading to amyloid-beta production." ApoE binding to ApoER2 *triggers* endocytosis of APP/BACE1 (ApoE4 > ApoE2/E3 for Aβ production) — internalisation promoted, recycling impedance not tested. Exclusion upheld.
5. DOI: 10.1073/pnas.0508693102 — 2005 — Ye S — "Apolipoprotein (apo) E4 enhances amyloid beta peptide production in cultured neuronal cells: apoE structure as a potential therapeutic target." ApoE4 *enhances* APP (cargo) recycling via the LRP pathway more than ApoE3 — opposite direction to H4, and the recycling measured is the cargo's, not the receptor's. Exclusion upheld.
6. DOI: 10.1371/journal.pone.0029385 — 2012 — Laatsch A — "Low density lipoprotein receptor-related protein 1 dependent endosomal trapping and recycling of apolipoprotein E." LRP1-dependent trapping/recycling of apoE *itself* (the cargo) in hepatoma cells, with no apoE isoform comparison anywhere in the abstract. Exclusion upheld.
7. DOI: 10.1016/j.celrep.2020.108224 — 2020 — Narayan P — "PICALM Rescues Endocytic Defects Caused by the Alzheimer's Disease Risk Factor APOE4." Generic endocytic defect in APOE4 iPSC astrocytes rescued by PICALM; no specific recycling receptor, no recycling measurement, no binding attribution. Exclusion upheld. (Also re-checked and upheld without separate listing: Huang 2019 neuronal signalling, Guttman 2010 ApoE–LRP interface structure with no isoform comparison, Wen 2011 VPS35/BACE1, Tammineni 2017 retromer trafficking in mutant-hAPP neurons, Fote 2022 isoform-dependent trafficking of apoE itself, Li 2012 Aβ cargo trafficking, Chan 2016 insulin signalling without receptor-trafficking measurement, Sawmiller 2019 apoE–APP interaction, Gordts 2012 LRP1 recycling in apoE-*deficient* mice, Perdigão 2021 BIN1/BACE1 recycling — none tests APOE4 binding to a recycling receptor impeding its recycling.)
