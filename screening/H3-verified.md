# H3 Verification — Screener B (independent second pass)

**Hypothesis H3:** "In some types of non-Alzheimer's, non-aged human brain cells, APOE4 undergoes increased aggregation in the endo-lysosome compared with APOE3."

**Verification criterion applied:** the paper is an experimental study that directly tests APOE4 vs APOE3/APOE2, or an APOE4 manipulation, on aggregation of the apoE protein itself — aggregation/oligomerization/fibrillization of apoE measured in endosomes/lysosomes, or in brain cells with endo-lysosomal localization. Model systems count. Reviews/meta-analyses, GWAS-only, clinical/epidemiological correlation-only studies, editorials, protocols, and amyloid-β/tau aggregation studies in which apoE aggregation itself is not measured are excluded. Verification was made from the abstracts in `work/searches2/H3.json` and `work/searches2/H3r2.json` only (plus `work/searches2/starting_refs.json` for the starting reference). Adjudication rule for the borderline cases: confirm only if the abstract supports endo-lysosomal localization of the apoE aggregates (or the aggregation is measured in cells in a way that is endo-lysosomal by design); hold out as **Disputed** if localization (or the isoform test) is genuinely unestablished and arguable both ways; exclude if the paper clearly does not measure apoE aggregation in an endo-lysosomal context.

**Starting reference check (prominent):** Kaji S, et al., Immunity 2024, PMID 39419029, DOI 10.1016/j.immuni.2024.09.014, "Apolipoprotein E aggregation in microglia initiates Alzheimer's disease pathology by seeding β-amyloidosis" — **NOT CONFIRMED: absent from the search pool (retrieval gap, independently verified by B: neither PMID 39419029 nor DOI 10.1016/j.immuni.2024.09.014 appears in either run) and NOT ELIGIBLE for the Confirmed list on its abstract — held out as DISPUTED (see Disputed #3).** Its abstract is the strongest endo-lysosomal-localization evidence in the entire exercise — fibrillary APOE aggregates that "triggered Aβ amyloidosis within the endo-lysosomal system of microglia," via "endocytic uptake and aggregation of APOE by microglia" — but it reports **no APOE4-vs-APOE3/APOE2 comparison and no APOE4-specific manipulation**: no isoform is named anywhere in the abstract, and the isoform carried by the HaloTag-tagged APOE knockin mouse is not stated. The isoform test that H3 requires therefore cannot be verified from the abstract. A second mismatch: the aggregates were identified in mice with Aβ amyloidosis and in human AD brain autopsies, whereas H3 is framed for non-Alzheimer's, non-aged human brain cells. Full-text adjudication of the knockin isoform is recommended before any final inclusion; on abstract evidence alone it does not meet the eligibility rule as written.

## Disputed

Each of the following is genuinely arguable both ways on its abstract and is held out, not counted as confirmed. Full-text adjudication is recommended for all three.

1. **DOI: 10.1523/jneurosci.1400-19.2019 — 2019 — Rawat V — "ApoE4 Alters ABCA1 Membrane Trafficking in Astrocytes."** (PMID: 31641056; included by A, borderline.)
   Reason: apoE aggregation itself is measured isoform-specifically — human CSF from APOE ε4/ε4 carriers shows a greater percentage of aggregated ApoE protein than CSF from ε3/ε3 carriers, and enhancing ABCA1 activity reduces ApoE and ABCA1 aggregation in the hippocampus of ApoE4-targeted replacement mice — and the paper's mechanism is endosomal (ApoE4 raises ARF6 and traps ABCA1 in late endosomes in astrocytes). However, the aggregates themselves are never localized inside endosomes/lysosomes in the abstract: the CSF aggregates are extracellular by design, the hippocampal aggregation readout is not compartment-resolved, and the late-endosome finding concerns ABCA1 trafficking, not the apoE aggregates. Whether the measured apoE aggregation is endo-lysosomal is genuinely unestablished and arguable both ways.

2. **DOI: 10.1016/j.neuron.2017.09.003 — 2017 — Zhao N — "Apolipoprotein E4 Impairs Neuronal Insulin Signaling by Trapping Insulin Receptor in the Endosomes."** (PMID: 28957663; excluded by A as a near-miss; upgraded to Disputed by B.)
   Reason: the abstract explicitly couples the two halves of H3 in neurons and apoE-targeted replacement mice — apoE4 traps the insulin receptor in endosomes (directly measured), and "in aging brains, the increased apoE4 aggregation and compromised endosomal function further exacerbate the inhibitory effects of apoE4 on insulin signaling." B disagrees with A's flat exclusion because increased apoE4 aggregation is stated in the results narrative alongside directly measured endosomal dysfunction, not only in the introduction. But the abstract does not make clear that apoE aggregation was itself assayed as an outcome (no aggregation measurement or APOE4-vs-APOE3 aggregation comparison is described), and the aggregation clause concerns aging brains, whereas H3 is framed for non-aged cells. Arguable both ways; held out.

3. **DOI: 10.1016/j.immuni.2024.09.014 — 2024 — Kaji S — "Apolipoprotein E aggregation in microglia initiates Alzheimer's disease pathology by seeding β-amyloidosis."** (PMID: 39419029; starting reference — manually added for assessment; absent from the search pool, retrieval gap.)
   Reason: see the prominent starting-reference verdict above. Endo-lysosomal localization of apoE aggregation in microglia is explicit and central — the best localization evidence available — but no APOE isoform comparison or APOE4 manipulation is reported in the abstract, so the APOE4-vs-APOE3 test required by the eligibility rule cannot be verified; the experimental contexts (Aβ-amyloidosis mice, human AD autopsies) also sit outside H3's non-Alzheimer's, non-aged framing. Held out pending full-text confirmation of the knockin isoform and any isoform-stratified analysis.

## Borderline-excluded

1. **DOI: 10.1126/scitranslmed.abc9375 — 2021 — Liu CC — "APOE3-Jacksonville (V236E) variant reduces self-aggregation and risk of dementia."** (PMID: 34586832; included by A, borderline; **excluded by B — B overturns A's inclusion.**)
   Reason: apoE self-aggregation is the direct experimental outcome and the isoform relevance is real — the V236E (APOE3-Jacksonville) variant reduces APOE aggregation and enhances lipidation relative to APOE3 in human brains and in cellular (astrocyte) and biochemical assays, and the same substitution reduces APOE4 aggregation. But unlike Rawat 2019, whose abstract at least ties aggregation to an endosomal mechanism in the same study, this abstract contains no endo-lysosomal element at all: no endosome, lysosome, or endo-lysosomal localization of the aggregates is mentioned, and the aggregation assays (brain tissue, astrocyte expression, biochemical) are not endo-lysosomal by design. The paper therefore clearly does not measure apoE aggregation in an endo-lysosomal context on its abstract, and is excluded rather than held out.

A excluded each of the following six near-misses; B re-read the abstracts and agrees with each exclusion, for the reasons given. None is counted as confirmed or disputed.

2. **DOI: 10.1038/s41467-024-49028-z — 2024 — Xia Z — "Co-aggregation with Apolipoprotein E modulates the function of Amyloid-β in Alzheimer's disease."** (PMID: 38824138.) ApoE–Aβ co-aggregates are quantified in APOE4-homozygote frontal cortex (~50% of the mass of diffusible Aβ aggregates), but the aggregation process measured throughout is Aβ aggregation/fibrillation, clearance and toxicity; apoE figures as an isoform- and lipidation-dependent modulator of Aβ, and there is no endo-lysosomal setting. Excluded under the Aβ-aggregation rule; A's exclusion upheld.
3. **DOI: 10.1074/jbc.m112109200 — 2002 — Ji ZS — "Apolipoprotein E4 potentiates amyloid beta peptide-induced lysosomal leakage and apoptosis in neuronal cells."** (PMID: 11912196.) Intracellular apoE (presumed, in the authors' words, to be in endosomes/lysosomes) is required for the isoform-specific effect, and apoE4 disrupts phospholipid vesicles more than apoE3/E2 — but the aggregation manipulated and measured is that of Aβ1-42; apoE aggregation itself is not assayed. A's exclusion upheld.
4. **DOI: 10.1074/jbc.m506646200 — 2006 — Ji ZS — "Reactivity of apolipoprotein E4 and amyloid beta peptide: lysosomal stability and neurodegeneration."** (PMID: 16298992.) Lysosomal-pH-dependent conversion of apoE4 to a membrane-disrupting molten globule is a conformational-reactivity finding, not a measurement of apoE aggregation; the paper itself shows aggregation is essential for the lysosomal-leakage effect and that the aggregation in question is Aβ's (multimer-forming "Arctic" vs monomeric GM6 Aβ). A's exclusion upheld.
5. **DOI: 10.1016/j.cell.2024.10.027 — 2025 — Guo JL — "Decreased lipidated ApoE-receptor interactions confer protection against pathogenicity of ApoE and its lipid cargoes in lysosomes."** (PMID: 39532095.) Endolysosomal targeting of lipidated ApoE is directly studied in human neurons with an ApoE4 > ApoE3 > ApoE2 allelic series, but the lysosomal outcomes measured are lipid cargo toxicity — lipofuscinosis and lysosomal accumulation of tau fibrils — not aggregation of apoE itself. A's exclusion upheld.
6. **DOI: 10.3389/fnins.2017.00702 — 2017 — Nuriel T — "The Endosomal-Lysosomal Pathway Is Dysregulated by APOE4 Expression in Vivo."** (PMID: 29311783.) APOE4-specific endosomal-lysosomal dysregulation is shown in vivo (transcriptomics; enlarged early endosomes in APOE4-targeted replacement mice), but no apoE aggregation of any kind is measured. A's exclusion upheld.
7. **DOI: 10.1523/jneurosci.5633-07.2008 — 2008 — Belinson H — "Activation of the amyloid cascade in apolipoprotein E4 transgenic mice induces lysosomal activation and neurodegeneration resulting in marked cognitive deficits."** (PMID: 18448646.) Neprilysin inhibition produces intracellular accumulation of Aβ and apoE with lysosomal activation, isoform-specifically in apoE4 mice — but the aggregation measured is Aβ nucleation/aggregation; apoE accumulation is not shown to be apoE aggregation. A's exclusion upheld.

## H3 full-text adjudication (2026-11-18)

Full-text pass over the three Disputed papers, applying the same three-element test used at screening: (a) aggregation of apoE itself measured; (b) an APOE4-vs-APOE3/APOE2 comparison or an APOE4-specific manipulation; (c) the aggregates localized to the endo-lysosomal system. A paper is confirmed only if all three are established by the text actually read. One supplementary Europe PMC search was also run (see end of this section).

### 1. Rawat 2019 — DOI 10.1523/jneurosci.1400-19.2019 (PMID 31641056) — STILL HELD OUT

**Text retrieved:** full text via PMC6880458 (PMC article HTML, saved as `work/fulltext2/PMC6880458.html`, text as `PMC6880458_full.txt`; the Europe PMC fullTextXML on file carries front matter/abstract only).

- **(a) YES.** ApoE aggregation itself is directly assayed by guanidine-HCl fractionation and Western blotting: "The relative amount of aggregated ABCA1 and ApoE were significantly increased in ApoE4 primary astrocytes compared with ApoE3 primary astrocytes (Fig. 4D, E)"; human CSF showed a "greater percentage of aggregated ApoE protein in CSF of ε4/ε4 carriers (2.5-fold increased) compared with ε3/ε3 carriers (Fig. 4J, K)."
- **(b) YES.** Isoform comparisons throughout (rApoE4 vs rApoE3 in BHK cells, ApoE4- vs ApoE3-targeted-replacement astrocytes and mouse hippocampus, ε4/ε4 vs ε3/ε3 human CSF), plus an APOE4-directed manipulation (the ABCA1 agonist CS-6253 reduces ApoE4 aggregation in vivo).
- **(c) NO.** The aggregation assays are run on whole preparations — per the Methods, "guanidine-HCl (GnHCl) soluble fractions were collected from rApoE isoforms, BHK cells, primary astrocytes, hippocampal homogenates or CSF" — never on endosomal/lysosomal fractions, and the paper's only colocalization imaging is of ABCA1 against LAMP2/Rab11, not of apoE aggregates. Endo-lysosomal localization of apoE aggregates appears only as cited background/speculation from Morrow et al. 2002 ("Lipid-poor, aggregated ApoE4 in the acidic endosomal compartment (Morrow et al., 2002) provides a possible mechanism for the endosomal congestion observed in ApoE4"), not as this paper's measurement.
- **Decision: still held out.** Full text confirms (a) and (b) but does not establish (c); the aggregates measured are extracellular (CSF) or compartment-unresolved (whole lysates/homogenates).

### 2. Zhao 2017 — DOI 10.1016/j.neuron.2017.09.003 (PMID 28957663) — STILL HELD OUT

**Text retrieved:** full text via Europe PMC fullTextXML, PMC5621659 (`work/fulltext2/PMC5621659.xml` / `.txt`).

- **(a) YES (full text only; not visible in the abstract).** Aggregation of apoE itself is assayed in mouse cortex (Fig. 7): brains were fractionated into RIPA-soluble and guanidine (GND, detergent-insoluble) fractions, and "the insoluble apoE and IR in GND fractions, which contain the aggregated proteins, were detected by ELISA."
- **(b) YES.** ApoE3-TR vs apoE4-TR mice: "the level of insoluble apoE was significantly increased in aged apoE4-TR mice compared with that in young mice (Figure 7E)"; "The increase of insoluble apoE4 in aged brain suggests that apoE4 might be more prone to aggregation with aging compared to apoE3."
- **(c) NO.** The aggregation readout is whole-cortex fractionation; no endosomal/lysosomal fractionation or colocalization of the apoE aggregates is reported. The paper's endosomal experiments concern insulin-receptor trapping in endosomes and Rab5-positive endosomal enlargement with aging; an endosomal site for apoE4 aggregation is offered only as Discussion speculation citing prior work ("The acidic environment in the early endosomal compartment has been proposed to enhance apoE4 self-aggregation (Morrow et al., 2002)"). The aggregation effect is also aging-dependent (aged brains), whereas H3 is framed for non-aged cells.
- **Decision: still held out.** (a) and (b) are established by the full text, (c) is not.

### 3. Kaji 2024 — DOI 10.1016/j.immuni.2024.09.014 (PMID 39419029; H3 starting reference) — CONFIRMED

**Text retrieved:** no PMCID exists (NCBI idconv: "Identifier not found in PMC"), so the full text was obtained from the publisher's CC-BY open-access version via the LMU Munich institutional repository (submitted version, 62 pp. incl. supplement; saved as `work/fulltext2/Kaji2024.pdf`, text as `Kaji2024.txt` / `Kaji2024_flat.txt`).

- **HaloTag knockin isoform (the question Screener B flagged): the knockin carries MOUSE APOE, not any human isoform.** "We generated transgenic knockin mice expressing a HaloTag fused to mouse APOE (mAPOE) (ApoE-Halo)"; the allele is B6J-Apoe<em2(HaloTag7)Bwef>, i.e. HaloTag7 inserted before the stop codon of the endogenous mouse Apoe gene. The HaloTag model therefore contains no APOE4-vs-APOE3 contrast — but the paper supplies the isoform test in other arms (below).
- **(a) YES.** Aggregation of APOE itself is measured directly and repeatedly: fibrillary, sarkosyl-insoluble APOE aggregates purified from 5xFAD and human AD brains, and recombinant APOE aggregation tracked by FPLC, RT-QuIC, CD spectroscopy and EM.
- **(b) YES.** Direct human isoform comparison of APOE aggregation in vitro: "FPLC analysis detected the emergence of APOE4 in the higher molecular weight fractions, indicating aggregate formation (Figure S4F)"; "When APOE3 was analyzed similarly, we did not observe altered W222/W208 ratio, increase in Thioflavin T fluorescence intensity, or drastic changes in molecular weight determined by FPLC (Figures 3E and S4G)." In vivo, human APOE4-knockin 5xFAD mice show higher MX04+ plaque load than APOE3-knockin controls (Fig. 3A), and injected recombinant APOE4 aggregates seed plaque formation whereas identically preincubated APOE3 does not (Fig. 3G).
- **(c) YES.** The paper's central localization claim, with marker evidence: "APOE aggregation occurs within the endo-lysosomal system of microglia" (Highlights); in 5xFAD ApoE-Halo brain, "APOE-Halo+MX04+ structures inside of IBA1+ cells were often associated with CD68" (lysosomal marker; Fig. 4), MX04+ aggregates co-stain with LAMP1 in microglia cultures, and lysosomal dysfunction promotes their formation.
- **Decision: CONFIRMED.** All three elements are established by the full text. Caveats recorded for the extraction: the isoform contrast (b) and the endo-lysosomal localization (c) come from different experimental arms — (b) from cell-free recombinant protein and knockin/seeding experiments, (c) from mouse-APOE models and microglia cultures without an isoform arm — and the in vivo contexts are amyloid-bearing mice and AD autopsies rather than the non-Alzheimer's, non-aged human cells of the statement's framing (model-system evidence, which the eligibility rule accepts; the framing gap is reflected in the findings' relevance and directness scores). Extracted to `work/extraction2/H3.json` (1 paper, 3 findings).
